History Recapped does NOT use the SportsStoriesAnimated format (illustrated beats + 1 footage cut at the narrative climax). The channel is **100% real archival footage** with our narrator on top.

**Why:** Tried sports-style on the first render (1 footage cut + 18 illustrated war-history-book plates, 63s). User rejected: "no suspense format in this type of shorts". The illustrated plates read as cheap when the channel premise is "real footage of real history". Sports works because the goal/celebration animation is the artistic interpretation of a moment we then cut to the real broadcast — but for a 60s war recap, the illustrations are filler between the only thing that matters: the actual archive.

**How to apply:**
- For History Recapped (and likely any documentary-archival channel): pick 5-7 footage windows from HD source(s), concat with blurred-letterbox 9:16, mute source audio, overlay our narration + word captions + inline national-flag emojis. NO animations, NO `black_intro: true` suspense pads.
- Bypass `make_shorts.py` for footage-only renders — image-gen wastes ~30 min on a M2 Max for beats whose images are never used. The generic `historyrecapped/scripts/render_footage_only.py` drives `pipeline.audio.synthesize` + `pipeline.beats.transcribe_words` + `pipeline.beats.split_into_beats` + `pipeline.compose.prerender_word_captions` + ffmpeg trim/concat/burn end-to-end (~1-2 min vs 30 min). Channel YAML's `render_style: footage_only` makes `workers/heavy/render_short.py` route through it automatically when cron drives the render.
- **Window selection requires 1fps verification.** Picking windows from a coarse 60s-cell montage repeatedly lands on talking-head presenters or modern text-overlay B-roll. Before committing in_s/out_s in `historyrecapped/shotlist/<slug>.json`, sample each candidate minute at 1fps (`ffmpeg -ss N -t 60 -i src -vf fps=1,tile=10x6 grid.png`) and read the grid. Pointe du Hoc v0 had 2 of 7 windows land on talking heads — v1 fixed after sampling 7 candidate regions at 1fps.
- Cut-aware caption clamping is mandatory: when stitching N footage windows into one timeline, word captions whose display would naturally extend past a scene cut must be clamped to end 100ms BEFORE the cut. Otherwise a caption from the previous scene visibly persists into the next, which reads as a glitch.
- Source-audio mix should be 0.0 (mute source). Unlike sports — where the broadcast commentator's call IS the payoff — documentary sources have their own competing narration that conflicts with ours.
- Inline emoji flag composite: pull twemoji PNGs (`cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/72x72/<codepoint>.png`, CC-BY-4.0) and side-paste them onto each `word_NNNN.png` BEFORE burning. Apple Color Emoji.ttc renders blank through Pillow's freetype on macOS (sbix bitmap incompatibility) — don't waste time trying to make it work.
- **Window-duration budget vs narration WPM.** `_burn_video` tpads the silent video with the LAST FRAME if narration > silent_duration, which produces a frozen-tail glitch at the closer. Kokoro/am_michael at speed 1.0 runs ~135 wpm; bumping `tts_speed: 1.2` brings it to ~165 wpm. For 155-167-word historyrecapped narrations, budget total windows for at least narration_wpm × words / 60 + 3s buffer. The 2026-05-04 batch ran 7 × 8.5s = 59.5s windows; at Kokoro 1.0 wpm we saw 5-15s of last-frame freeze, fixed by 7 × 10s windows or speed=1.2.
- Re-rendering after a narration edit: clear `narration.wav`, `narration.voice.json`, `beats.json`, `prompts.json`, and `word_*.png` from `historyrecapped/cache/<slug>/`. Word PNGs are keyed by index, not content hash, so stale ones survive and produce mismatched captions otherwise.
- First upload to a brand-new YouTube channel takes 30-60min to second-pass-process even for a 50s Short. Not a bug, just YouTube's anti-spam pass on accounts with no upload history. Don't delete + reupload (deletion penalises channel reputation).
